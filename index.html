Six joints in the pipeline

How capital moves from a depositor's wallet to a buyback.

  1. Launch a coin and its agent

    Create a token paired with its own automated liquidity management agent.

  2. Fund a liquidity vault

    Users deposit SOL, USDC or supported tokens into a managed vault.

  3. Choose a strategy

    Concentrated liquidity, dynamic rebalancing, volatility-adjusted ranges or yield optimization.

  4. Automate the liquidity

    The agent deploys and rebalances positions across Raydium, Orca and Meteora.

  5. Generate revenue

    Positions collect trading fees. The protocol earns management and performance fees.

  6. Buy back the coin

    A portion of realized net protocol revenue funds buybacks of the associated token.

Liquidity management factory

Anyone can launch a tokenized liquidity management business without managing positions by hand. Say a community launches its coin with an agent that specializes in SOL/USDC pools. Users deposit into its vault, and the agent automatically:

  • Deploys liquidity into the pools it selected
  • Adjusts price ranges as market conditions change
  • Reinvests collected fees when it pays to
  • Moves liquidity between pools within preset risk limits
  • Tracks realized profit and routes eligible revenue into buybacks

What automation can't fix

An agent can manage liquidity well. It can't make these go away, and the Range desk shows them on purpose.

Impermanent lossEvery rebalance locks in some of it.
Adverse selectionInformed traders still pick off stale ranges.
Smart-contract riskAudits reduce it. Nothing removes it.
Transaction costsEach move costs something, even on Solana.
No guaranteesFee income and buybacks are not guaranteed.
